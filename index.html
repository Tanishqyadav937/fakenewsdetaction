Spotting Misinformation Red Flags

Key diagnostic rules to identify fake news before sharing online

🚩

1. Sensational & Emotional Headlines

Fake news uses ALL CAPS, exaggerated shock words ("DOCTORS HATE THIS!"), and emotional bait to trigger immediate impulse sharing before critical evaluation.

🕵️

2. Missing Byline & Named Sources

Authentic reporting lists clear journalist names and verifiable institution citations. Fake articles rely on vague phrases like "Scientists claim" or "Leaked documents show".

🖼️

3. Manipulated & Out-of-Context Media

Check images using reverse image search. Misinformation frequently recycles old disaster photos or AI-generated composite visuals to fabricate crisis events.

🔄

4. Circular Reporting & Echo Chambers

False claims cite obscure blogs that in turn cite the original claim. Always trace the primary source of the statement to confirm validity.
